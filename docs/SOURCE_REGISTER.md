# Gajendra Moksham — source register and adaptation log

Verified against the public Vedabase chapter texts on **2026-11-22**. Reference edition: **Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam Canto 8, Chapters 2–4**. This is an original child-friendly retelling, **not** a translation of the linked English edition and not a claim that every Hindu telling has identical details.

## Chapter anchors

- [8.2 — Gajendra's crisis](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/8/2/): Trikūṭa mountain; Ṛtumat garden; elephant herd and lake; crocodile grasps Gajendra's leg; herd cannot rescue him; struggle extends for a thousand years; water favors the crocodile; Gajendra seeks divine shelter.
- [8.3 — Gajendra's prayer](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/8/3/): a prayer remembered from a former birth by divine grace; address to the Supreme; Lord Hari/Nārāyaṇa arrives **on Garuḍa**; the suffering elephant lifts a **lotus with his trunk**; the Lord draws both elephant and crocodile from the lake and ends the crocodile's animal life with the disc.
- [8.4 — Gajendra's deliverance](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/8/4/): Hūhū the Gandharva, formerly cursed by **Devala**; Gajendra formerly **King Indradyumna**, cursed by **Agastya** during his silent worship; Hūhū restored to Gandharva form; Gajendra receives **sārūpya-mukti** (four-armed spiritual form); the Lord gives blessings, sounds His conch and departs on Garuḍa with Gajendra.

## Particular source-fidelity guardrails

1. **Gajendra's state:** He is an elephant in the living action. The divine remembrance/prayer can use a stylized inner voice or voice-over; animal mouth-synced human dialogue is an editorial device, not a scriptural assertion.
2. **Duration of struggle:** The source explicitly says a **thousand years** ([8.2.29](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/8/2/29/)). Convey this through narrator/time montage rather than repetitive attack footage or a misleading ordinary clock.
3. **Lotus timing:** Gajendra raises a lotus **when he sees Nārāyaṇa on Garuḍa**, not as a magical object that independently summons Him ([8.3.31–32](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/8/3/)).
4. **Nature of rescue:** The Lord lifts Gajendra **with the crocodile** and uses Sudarśana; a cutaway may make this safe for children without falsely changing the event ([8.3.33](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/8/3/33/)).
5. **Two curses:** Hūhū/Devala and Indradyumna/Agastya have different situations; do not collapse the two or swap sages ([8.4.3–12](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/8/4/)).
6. **Moksha:** Gajendra attains a divine **four-armed** form and accompanies the Lord; do not portray this merely as walking home in elephant form ([8.4.6,13](https://vedabase.io/en/library/sb/8/4/)).
7. **Prayer selection:** The original prayer is extended and theological. Child-accessible paraphrase must retain its devotion and not be passed off as exact scripture or a verbatim English translation. Verse recitation should use independently reviewed Sanskrit, pronunciation and permissions.

## Scriptwriting policy / material deviations (planned, not written)

- Original connective scenes, animal behavioral beats, dialogue, cross-cutting and camera business must be labeled **original dramatization** in each authored episode.
- The narration may follow the scripture's reveal of previous lives **after** rescue; dramatized flashbacks in S01E05 are a production presentation, not a claim the on-scene animals speak.
- Avoid declaring Gajendra's suffering divine punishment for pride; strength and its limits are emotional framing, not a manufactured causal explanation.
- Do not portray the crocodile as eternally evil: Hūhū is restored following the crocodile's death.
- No song, chanting recording, score, translation, or artwork may be copied from a third-party production without rights review.
