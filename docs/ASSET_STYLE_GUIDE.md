## Design gates

- **Elephant:** Indian elephant proportions, four legs and one trunk; ear/trunk/tusk geometry stays consistent across angles. Gajendra is visually recognizable within the herd. No human hands in ordinary elephant sequences.
- **Crocodile:** semi-submerged body, believable jaw/teeth and tail; no human face or lip-flap. Screen direction must preserve which leg is gripped.
- **Divine form:** Nārāyaṇa's approved screen anatomy and held iconographic objects are stable in all frames; the later `GAJENDRA_LIBERATED` spiritual four-armed form is a **separate design**, not an elephant with extra arms.
- **Garuḍa:** plausible wing-to-body proportions and consistent passenger positioning. Reference set should support wide, three-quarter and front arrival shots.
- **Land/water:** reusable master layout; near bank, deeper water, entry path, lotus area and distant Trikūṭa peaks remain geographically coherent.
- **Flashbacks:** clearly distinct visual time/places; do not let a reused backdrop make Hūhū's water episode appear identical to the crocodile attack.

## Animation and sound

Prioritize naturalistic animal action and expressive head/trunk/ear motion; use A2V talking portraits only for appropriate human/divine speakers and **after** evaluating the shot. For Gajendra's spiritual prayer use narrator/inner voice with visual reaction, not mandatory lipsync. Use separate stems for voice, ambience, sound effects and original/licensed score.
