# Gajendra Moksham — series bible (planning)

## Central idea and emotional motion

**Abundance → sudden danger → exhausting struggle → honest surrender → divine rescue → revealed causes → liberation and gratitude.**

A majestic elephant king enjoys a beautiful life with his herd, but physical strength cannot answer every problem. He remembers to offer wholehearted prayer. Nārāyaṇa comes to him in compassion. The story ultimately concerns **bhakti and moksha**, not a stronger animal defeating a weaker one.

The crocodile is genuinely dangerous in the lake; only later does the account reveal he is Hūhū, under a curse. Treat both beings with scriptural dignity.

## Character continuity

- **GAJENDRA:** impressive Indian elephant king; protective and alert; no crown or talking-mouth caricature. At first he trusts physical power; when abandoned by ordinary means, he turns to remembered devotion. The prayer can be narrated internally without making the elephant's lips move like a human actor.
- **CROCODILE:** formidable wild crocodile in water, initially not a comic villain or named person. No elaborate human speech. Tracks with **HŪHŪ** in the revealed former-life account.
- **NĀRĀYAṆA/HARI/VIṢṆU:** one divine identity with these names, depicted consistently; compassionate intervention is immediate once He comes. Four arms with conch/disc/mace/lotus are an iconographic **screen design**, not permission to invent a new weapon-use scene.
- **GARUḌA:** Lord's bird carrier, imposing but benevolent, stable wing pattern, body size and passenger layout.
- **HŪHŪ:** Gandharva of an earlier life, later released from crocodile form. Regret at the sage's rebuke; not an unrelated character.
- **DEVALA ṚṢI:** sage who curses Hūhū after a disruptive act in water.
- **INDRADYUMNA:** former devoted human king, retired in the Malaya Hills and observing a vow of silence; same soul later born as Gajendra, but do not merge their physical designs.
- **AGASTYA ṚṢI:** sage arriving with disciples, displeased at Indradyumna's failure to greet him while silent in worship.
- **HERD:** elephants around Gajendra. They try unsuccessfully to rescue him; their behavior should read as natural animal behavior rather than invented animal conversations.
- **NARRATOR:** an editorial voice, not proof of an on-screen named character. Optionally present the Śukadeva–Parīkṣit framing in a later deliberate source-reviewed decision.

## Geographic continuity

- **Trikūṭa:** three distinctive peaks (gold, silver, iron as named in scripture), recognizable waterfalls and forest silhouette; don't substitute an ordinary modern Himalayan landmark.
- **Ṛtumat garden:** Varuṇa's lush garden below Trikūṭa; the lake has clear surface, flowers and deep crocodile habitat.
- **Lake geography:** one consistent inlet, land bank and central deep-water region. Gajendra's initial push toward land must remain geographically readable.
- **Gandharva flashback:** a clearly different water setting when Hūhū pulls Devala's leg.
- **Indradyumna flashback:** Malaya Hills hermitage with quiet worship; do not cut to the lake as if it is the same timeline.
- **Divine resolution:** Garuḍa's landing, lotus offer, rescue, spiritual form and departure are distinct states requiring separate visual frames and accurate continuity.

## Dramatic rules

1. Each episode must alter the situation; do not pad episodes with repeated tugging.
2. Natural sounds and limited inner voice maintain a credible elephant protagonist.
3. A one-thousand-year struggle is not narrated as ordinary seasonal weather for a few months.
4. The complete extended prayer may need an artful, respectful selection; never reduce all prayer to only “save me.”
5. Nārāyaṇa must **arrive on Garuḍa**, Gajendra must **offer a lotus**, and the rescue uses **Sudarśana**.
6. The crocodile's physical death and Hūhū's restoration must both remain intelligible without gore.
7. Gajendra's four-armed spiritual form is deliberate, reverent and distinct from the elephant's normal body.
8. All direct quoted dialogue/voices in future scripts are original screenplay unless marked as independently checked source verse.

## Production style

16:9 cinematic illustrated Indian devotional storytelling, consistent original designs and readable silhouettes. Avoid imitating any named animation studio. Use carefully authored stills, short I2V with start/end frames, compositing, and A2V only where it makes anatomical and narrative sense. Text-first and audio-first approval precede expensive media generation.

**Status:** editorial bible, not an approved animation design or authored episode script.
